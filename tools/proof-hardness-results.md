# Which functions were hardest to spec, and which hardest to prove?

Generated by `tools/proof_timeline.py` over the whole history (2545 commits,
2026-06-23 .. 2026-08-14, HEAD 7e449ea8), 185 function units.  Every number is
a git-history proxy; the "reading the data" section says where each one lies.

Reproduce:

```
tools/proof_timeline.py --sort=hard-proof --top=15        # composite
tools/proof_timeline.py --sort=focused --md               # any single metric
tools/proof_timeline.py --tsv --top=0 > hardness.tsv      # every column
```

## The short answer

**Hardest to prove:** `virtio_disk_rw`, `kernelvec`, `printk`, `kexec` (still
unlinked), `walk`, `itrunc`, `kfork`, `namex`.  Two different shapes of hard
live in that list -- see finding 1.

**Hardest to spec:** `writei`, then `iupdate`, `dirlink`, `log_write`, `bmap`,
`create`, `itrunc` -- essentially the whole fs-log / fs-sysfile
credited-contract campaign, plus `fileclose` and `main` from the earlier
proc-layer work.

**Overlap between the two top-15s is only 5 of 15** (`writei`, `dirlink`,
`itrunc`, `log_write`, `namex`): hard-to-prove and hard-to-spec are mostly
*different* functions.

## Reading the data

**1. There are two distinct kinds of hard proof, and only one of them looks
hard in the commit log.**  `kernelvec` (17 focused rework commits, 19
author-days, but only 1856 lines of proof) and `printk` (19 rework commits,
12.7k lines of non-sweep churn) were *ground out*: many small commits over
many days.  `namex` and `kexec` were the opposite -- `namex` went from first
design note to a linked 6481-line proof in two calendar days, with only 4
rework commits and 8072 lines of churn.  It was not easy; it was done in one
sustained burst by an agent campaign that front-loaded the design (eleven
decode layers and two pure grammar libraries landed before the proof opened).
Any metric based on elapsed time or commit counts under-rates burst work, and
any metric based on churn under-rates grind work; that is why the composite
mixes four components.

**2. The most reliable hardness signal in this repo is not the proof, it is
the spec that would not stop moving.**  `writei` has 10 focused spec edits
*after* it was first proven and linked, `dirlink` 9, `create` 7, `iupdate` and
`allocproc` 5 apiece.  Reading the subjects of those commits, they are one
story: the credited-contract campaign (`fs-sysfile` stages S2-S5, `fs-log`
G-*), in which the log-budget/credit accounting was re-threaded through
`writei` -> `dirlink` -> `create` again and again.  The proofs were rebuilt
each time -- `dirlink` also carries 11 focused proof-rework commits -- so on
this evidence the expensive mistake in this project was not a hard proof, it
was a contract that had to be renegotiated after its consumers existed.

**3. Forty percent of all proof-file churn is mechanical sweeps, and it accrues
with a function's age rather than its difficulty, so any un-filtered metric
partly ranks by birth date.**  Across 185 units, 240,821 lines of proof churn come
from commits touching more than 20 iris files (Phi-quantifier removal,
iNext -> bi.later_intro, dead-import sweeps, xv6 image bumps) against 365,108
lines of focused work.  A proof born in July has been swept a median of 21
times for a median 857 lines; one born in August, 11.5 times for 392 lines.
Before filtering, `cpuid` -- a two-instruction leaf --
showed 13 "author-days" and 812 lines of churn purely from being swept; after
filtering it shows 2 days and 57 lines.  Every leaf function had the same
inflation, which is the single biggest correction in this tool.

**4. Sixteen commits threw a finished proof away, and one contract change
caused most of them.**  Fourteen units have a deleted-and-rewritten proof
file.  The `hart-generic scheduler, stage 2` commit (0cfc6449, 2026-07-30)
alone deleted `virtio_disk_rw`'s entire eight-file part chain plus
`piperead`, which were then re-proven over the following days
("piperead: re-proven against the hart-generic contract").  `uartwrite` is the
only function whose proof was discarded in three *separate* commits.  Redos
are rare enough to read individually and are worth more than any aggregate.

**5. Nine functions were assumed inside other people's cones before they were
proven, and that hides real spec effort.**  `LinkIput.v` was created on
2026-08-06 in a commit titled "... and iput assumed"; `ProofIput.v` did not
exist until 2026-08-10.  The same pattern holds for `panic` (specced
2026-07-26, proven 2026-08-13 -- the longest spec-to-proof gap in the tree at
30 days), `balloc`, `kerneltrap`, `consoleread`, `consolewrite`, `consoleintr`,
`idup`, `allocpid`.  `iput` is the interesting case for the original question:
its design was discussed in the notes 11 days before `SpecIput.v` existed and
it was axiomatized for four days, yet its spec barely moved after it was
proven.  It was hard to *design*, not hard to keep stable -- which the
instability metric alone would have missed entirely.

## Caveats, honestly

* **The unit boundary is a naming convention, not semantics.**  Only 63% of
  iris/*.v churn lands on a `Spec<F>/Proof<F>/Link<F>/<F>Budget` file.  The
  other 37% is shared infrastructure *and* bespoke per-function libraries
  (`PathElems.v`, `DirentEnc.v`, `InodeRegion.v` for the namei cone;
  `VirtioDiskRwDefs.v`), so functions that carry a library are systematically
  under-counted.  `namex` is the worst offender: its true cost is higher than
  any column here shows.
* **Author-days are calendar days, not person-hours.**  Nearly all commits
  carry one of two author identities and agents work in parallel, so a day on
  which four functions advanced counts as a day for each.
* **The 5-file / 20-file commit-width cuts are arbitrary.**  Re-running with
  3/40 and 8/15 keeps `writei` first on spec-hardness in all three settings
  and keeps `virtio_disk_rw`, `walk`, `itrunc`, `kernelvec` and `writei` in
  the proof top-10 in all three, but shuffles positions 6-15 noticeably.
  Treat the tables as tiers, not as a ranking.
* **Notes-mention dates are word matches.**  A unit named after an ordinary
  English word gets an unreliable design-start date and is flagged
  `homonym?`; `main`, `walk`, `wakeup`, `entry` and `create` all carry it.
* **`create`, `kexec` and `user` are still unfinished** (no Link file), so
  their numbers are lower bounds and their `elapsed` is blank rather than
  large.

## Method

* Unit = one `iris/Spec<F>.v`; files attach by longest-prefix match, so
  `ProofNamexParts.v`, `ProofKforkB1..B7`, `ProofKexecSeam.v` and
  `WriteiBudget.v` land on the right function and `ProofNameiparent.v` does
  not land on `namei`.
* `spec_start` = earliest of (first spec-side file added) and (first design
  notes commit that mentions the function >= 3 times in the lines it adds).
* `proven` = when a `Link<F>.v` **and** a real proof file both exist, which
  corrects the nine "assumed first" cases.
* `focused` = commits touching <= 5 iris files that changed the proof after it
  was created; `sweeps` = commits touching > 20; author-days and `churn_work`
  count non-sweep commits only, `churn_all` counts everything.
* `instab` = focused spec-file commits dated after the proof landed.
* Composites (`hard-proof`, `hard-spec`) are percentile rank-sums of their
  component columns, so no single fat-tailed column dominates.
